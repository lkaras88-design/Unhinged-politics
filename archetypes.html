THE COMPLETE IDEOLOGICAL MENAGERIE

All 16 Archetypes

Because naturally you need to know what everyone else could have gotten.

Designed by L. Karas

The HOA Authoritarian

You believe liberty is sacred, but so is not parking a jet ski on the front lawn for nine months. Deep inside you lives a tiny zoning board with a clipboard, a ring camera, and absolutely no patience for Darren's unpermitted shed. Your political nightmare is anarchy; your personal nightmare is a trash can visible from the street after 7 p.m.

The Leave-Me-Alone Libertarian

Your governing philosophy is essentially: 'I don't know who authorized this, but please stop.' You distrust bureaucrats, busybodies, mandatory fun, and any form containing the phrase 'supporting documentation.' If society collapses, you will be on your porch saying, 'See? And they wanted a permit for this porch.'

The Benevolent Bureaucrat

You genuinely believe most human suffering could be reduced by competent institutions, clear procedures, and one PDF that people would simply READ. You are not hungry for power; you are hungry for a functioning appointment system. Your villain origin story is watching someone ignore the instructions and then blame the process.

The Revolutionary Barista

You have looked at the existing system, asked 'who benefits?', and ruined brunch. You are three rent increases and one unfair group-project grade away from seizing the means of cold brew production. Your revolution will have excellent graphic design, confusing committee meetings, and at least one person who insists the guillotine is 'problematic.'

The Traditionalist Uncle

You don't believe every old custom is holy, but you are deeply suspicious of any innovation introduced with the phrase 'hear me out.' Somewhere in your soul you have said, 'People used to know how to act,' possibly while operating technology that would have terrified your ancestors. Civilization peaked at an unspecified point when you were younger.

The Radical Centrist

You can see both sides with such supernatural efficiency that everyone else assumes you are avoiding commitment. You call it nuance; Twitter calls it cowardice. If offered a trolley problem, you will request the transportation department's full environmental-impact report before reluctantly splitting the difference.

The Populist Barstool Philosopher

Left versus right interests you less than normal people versus whoever has a lanyard, a consultant, and the phrase 'stakeholder engagement' in their bio. Your political theory begins with 'Here's what I don't understand...' and ends 45 minutes later with three strangers nodding. You may not know the CBO score, but you know somebody is getting screwed.

The Technocratic Spreadsheet Person

Ideology is cute; you brought data. You want the pilot program, confidence interval, cost-benefit analysis, and a dashboard that updates in real time. Democracy is important, obviously, but have we considered temporarily handing the country to the person who actually knows how VLOOKUP works?

The Commune Activities Director

You believe people are supposed to take care of each other, share burdens, and occasionally attend a meeting about the compost pile that should have been an email. Your utopia has mutual aid, communal meals, and a sign-up sheet nobody fills out correctly. You will abolish hierarchy immediately after assigning everyone chores.

The Law-and-Order Dad

Rules exist because somewhere, sometime, somebody did something incredibly stupid. You value safety, consequences, predictability, and the radical proposition that perhaps people should stop doing crimes. Your natural response to social collapse is to buy flashlights, check the locks, and ask why nobody respects consequences anymore.

The Institutional Liberal

You still believe democracy can work if everyone would stop setting the building on fire long enough to pass a bill. Rights, institutions, expertise, reform, and boring procedural safeguards matter to you. Your toxic trait is believing one more bipartisan commission might actually fix this.

The Disillusioned Moderate

You have seen enough politics to distrust anyone who sounds completely certain. You don't love the system; you just suspect most proposed replacements were designed during a podcast. Your ideology is exhausted competence: please fix the pothole, stop screaming, and for the love of God do not make me download another campaign app.

The Frontier Individualist

You believe people should handle their own business, solve their own problems, and own at least one object that can repair another object. You are not anti-community; you simply prefer community to arrive voluntarily and leave before dinner. If the apocalypse comes, everyone will suddenly remember you own tools.

TheNeighborhoodSocialDemocrat

You are fine with everyone doing their own thing until somebody cannot afford the ambulance. Then suddenly you have a tax proposal and a strongly worded opinion about public goods.

The Contrarian Goblin

Your ideology is whatever makes the most confident person in the room say, 'Wait, what?' You distrust consensus partly on principle and partly because irritating people is free. If everyone agrees the sky is blue, you are already opening seventeen tabs about atmospheric scattering and muttering, 'It's more complicated than that.'

The Civic Republican

You believe freedom requires citizens who actually participate, institutions worthy of trust, and some concept of the common good beyond 'I got mine.' You are the person who attends the town meeting voluntarily and somehow leaves with a committee assignment. The republic survives because people like you keep showing up while the rest of us mute the group email.
